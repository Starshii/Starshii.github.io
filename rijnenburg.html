Axolotters New Home

Description:
Explore the under construction neighbourhood of Rijnenburg while you grow new plants and trees for your bug-friends to enjoy! Take your time and leave your mark in this cosy exploration game about finding a better place for you.

Start of Project: 02 / 02 / 2026.
Development Time: 10 Weeks.
Team size: 3 Artists, 2 Designers, 1 Developer.
My roles: Level Design, Mechanic Design.
Software used: Unity, Paint.NET, Miro, Trello.

GOALS & CHALLENGES

The Goal

A game for a new neighborhood

For this project we got to make a game that introduces the future inhabitants of the upcoming neighborhood Rijnenburg to their new homes. Since everything is still under construction, the government wants future civilians to re-shape or add to the neighborhood. That's why the client wanted us sparking people's creativity by showing them what might be possible in their new living environment.

We also collaborated with UU students, who wanted the game to promote the sustainability aspect of Rijnenburg, as well as connecting people to the mystery of nature.

Challenge 1

Scoping

I was tasked to create a list of mechanics and systems to define the gameplay. Afterwards, the team picked the best ones to implement. Due to the many demands of the parties involved and the small scope of the project, we settled on the safest option because adhering to the clients wishes is more important than pushing my personal vision.

My approach

Focus on designing the feel

I merged my level design module with this project and focussed full time on the core feel of the game:

"A small creature exploring the great and beautiful under construction Rijnenburg after working hours"

I creatively iterated on the few systems and mechanics we had. The simplicity means the game is focussed on what its supposed to do.

The initial top down of the entire map. This is very different from the actual finalized level but we kept to the overall structure in terms of sectioning and sequence of mechanics as you can deduce from the gameplay showcase video.

Challenge 2

Communication through models

While trying to convey ideas to the other disciplines, The MDA framework felt a bit too design focussed which felt limiting. I wanted to convert ideas into concrete deliverables that made it clear what features we wanted in the game and what everyone's role was in creating those features.

My approach

The ViSDEC Model

Me and my fellow designer Ricardo Remmelzwaal created a prototype for a new model, derived from the MDA framework, but tailored towards the whole team. The idea was to create concrete deliverables by dissecting the game's features. While the model still needs testing and reworking, multiple designers from my school have already used it and are still using it, so I'd say there's definitely potential.

POSTMORTEM

What went well?

  • The core feel of the game resonated with playtesters.

  • The map is huge and varied both visually and mechanically.

  • Every part of the vision the team had for the level was fully realised

  • I learned a lot about open level design after receiving extensive feedback from Toby Canham, Pizzacocalypse's level designer.

What could I have done differently?

  • The 3C's could have been polished more, it still shows unintended behavior.

  • The introduction to the mechanics could have been done better, a lot of players did not grasp the basic mechanics well enough on their own.

  • I should have shifted my focus to the ending of the game sooner as it is a little confusing.

  • I could have spent more time on the sound design as there are no sound effects in the game.
